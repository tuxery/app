## Privacy

Tuxery has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking
cookies. Here is everything that happens to data about you when you
visit.

### What the server sees

Like any website, the server that answers your requests receives your
IP address, the page requested, the time and your browser's user agent.
Cloudflare, the host, keeps these as technical logs for Tuxery — used
only to keep the site working and to investigate errors or abuse — for at
most 7 days, then deletes them. They are not combined with anything else,
shared or used to profile anyone. Legal basis: Tuxery's legitimate
interest in running a working, secure site (GDPR article 6(1)(f)).

Cloudflare is a US company; its processing of personal data is covered
by its [data processing terms](https://www.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-customer-dpa/)
and the EU–US Data Privacy Framework.

### What stays in your browser

Your [settings](/settings/) — theme, operating system, preferred install
formats — are saved in your browser's local storage, so the site
remembers them. They never leave your device: Tuxery's server doesn't
read them. Clear your browser's site data to remove them. No cookie is
set.

### What other sites see

Pictures on Tuxery are loaded straight from where they are published:
app icons and screenshots from the stores and projects that host them
(Flathub, the Snap Store, GitHub...), background photos from Unsplash.
Your browser fetches them directly, so those servers see your IP
address, as they would if you visited them. Install buttons and links
take you to other sites, which have their own privacy policies.

### The catalog

Tuxery's catalog describes software, not people. Where a source names an
app's developer or maintainer, that public information is shown as the
source publishes it.

### Your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask for access to, correction or deletion of
data about you, or object to its processing, by writing to
[contact@tuxery.store](mailto:contact@tuxery.store). Since Tuxery itself
stores nothing about visitors beyond the short-lived logs above, most
requests are answered by that alone. You can also complain to the
[CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/plaintes), France's data protection
authority.
