# FAQ

## Is Tuxery an app store or an installer?

No. Tuxery is a catalog: it finds every way an app can be installed on
Linux and points you to the right one. Every Install button hands off to
the real source — Flathub, the Snap Store, your distribution's software
center or package manager, the developer's website. Tuxery never hosts a
package or runs installer code.

## Why isn't my app listed?

Usually one of these:

- **It's in a source Tuxery doesn't read yet** — see the
  [sources](/docs/sources/) list, and [propose one](https://github.com/tuxery/catalog/issues/new).
- **The filter took it for a non-app**: libraries, fonts, documentation,
  plugins and test packages are left out on purpose, and the rules
  sometimes get it wrong.
- **It's listed under another name**, merged into an app it belongs to:
  editions, other builds and add-ons appear on their main app's page.
  Search for the main app.

If it should be there, [open an issue](https://github.com/tuxery/catalog/issues/new)
with the app's name and where it's packaged.

## An app is merged wrongly, or shows up twice. How do I report it?

[Open an issue](https://github.com/tuxery/catalog/issues/new) on the
catalog repository with the address of the app's page on Tuxery and what
looks wrong. Merges are fixed with a reviewed rule, so the fix stays. An
in-site "flag this match" button is planned. See
[how sources are merged](/docs/merging/) for why mistakes happen.

## The category is wrong. Can I fix it?

Yes, the same way: an issue on the catalog repository. Categories come
from the app's own metadata when it has some, otherwise from rules and
language models — see the [data philosophy](/docs/philosophy/).

## Why do versions differ between sources?

Because each source ships its own. Stable distributions (Debian, Ubuntu
LTS) freeze versions for the life of a release and only add security
fixes; Flathub, Snap and rolling distributions follow new releases
closely. Tuxery shows each source's version as that source reports it,
so you know what you'd actually get. See
[which format to choose](/docs/formats/).

## Where do ratings come from?

From the people who rate apps on their own stores: GNOME's review
service (behind GNOME Software's and Flathub's stars) and GOG's reviews.
When an app has ratings from several sources, they are combined, weighted
by number of votes, and the per-source ratings are shown on the app's
page. Tuxery has no ratings of its own yet.

## How are "trending" and the other lists ordered?

By figures sources publish — never by payment. Details, and why an app
might not be trending, in [how rankings work](/docs/rankings/).

## How often is the data updated?

Every source is refetched weekly, and the catalog is republished from
that data. The date of the data currently shown is on the
[Status](/docs/status/) page.

## I'm the developer of an app. How do I fix its page?

Most of what Tuxery shows comes from your app's own metadata —
particularly its AppStream file (name, description, screenshots,
categories, license, release dates). Fixing it upstream fixes it on
Flathub, in software centers and on Tuxery at the next refresh. For
anything else — a wrong merge, a missing source, an edition shown the
wrong way — open an issue on the catalog repository. A way to claim your
app's page is planned; see [claim](/claim/).

## Can I submit an app or game?

Not through the site yet — a submission form is planned. For now,
[open an issue](https://github.com/tuxery/catalog/issues/new); the best
long-term route is getting the app into a source Tuxery reads (Flathub,
a distribution, the AUR...).

## Does Tuxery track me?

No analytics, no advertising, no account. Your settings (theme,
operating system, preferred formats) are stored in your own browser and
never sent anywhere. Like any website, the server that hosts it
(Cloudflare) keeps technical request logs.

## Can I reuse Tuxery's data?

Yes — see [open data](/docs/open-data/).

## How can I help?

See [how to contribute](/docs/contribute/): reporting bad data is the
most useful thing anyone can do, and the [per-source to-do](/docs/coverage/)
lists concrete work on each source.
